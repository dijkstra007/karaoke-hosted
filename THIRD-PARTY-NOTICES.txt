Device separation and model attribution
Source: frontend/lib/device-separation/NOTICE.md
======================================================================

The browser adapter uses `web-audio-separation` 0.3.0's public PffftSTFT implementation (MIT):
https://github.com/incidentist/web-audio-separation

It runs UVR-MDX-NET-Voc_FT using ONNX Runtime Web 1.29.0 (MIT):
https://github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime

Model source: Ultimate Vocal Remover and its developers, including Anjok07 and aufr33, and the MDX-Net work by Kuielab.
UVR's README asks third-party users of its models to honor MIT licensing and credit UVR and its developers:
https://github.com/Anjok07/ultimatevocalremovergui#license

No model weights are redistributed in this repository. At runtime, the browser downloads this immutable mirror revision:
https://huggingface.co/Blane187/all_public_uvr_models/resolve/fddec39677560e41e3194f24a9e4c4cd32ef0e83/UVR-MDX-NET-Voc_FT.onnx

Expected size: 66,762,490 bytes. SHA-256: 534b2070fcc7df514b13ef660dc8cbb328679c2374d04354a5c42bb14ecce111.
The mirror's model card labels the weights MIT; this does not document the provenance or licensing of every training recording.

The model expects 44,100 Hz stereo audio, a 7,680-sample Hann FFT, 1,024-sample hop, 3,072 frequency bins, and 256 time frames.
CPU FFT, inference overlap-add, section context, seam blending, and residual derivation preserve one common sample timeline.
